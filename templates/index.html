        How It Works

        From raw comments to a sentiment dashboard in six steps.

        1. 1

          YouTube Comments

          Comments are fetched with the YouTube Data API, or loaded from the sample dataset in Demo Mode.

        2. 2

          Text Preprocessing

          Lowercase, remove URLs and punctuation, fix contractions, drop stopwords but keep words like “not”.

        3. 3

          TF-IDF Vectorization

          Each comment becomes a list of numbers that show how important each word is.

        4. 4

          Machine Learning Model

          A Logistic Regression model, trained on labelled comments, scores the three classes.

        5. 5

          Sentiment Classification

          The class with the highest probability wins and that probability is the confidence.

        6. 6

          Analytics Dashboard

          Counts, charts, word insights and a searchable table summarise the audience opinion.

        About the Project

        The key ideas behind this application, in plain language.

        What is NLP?

        Natural Language Processing is the area of AI that teaches computers to read, understand and work with human language such as text and speech.

        What is sentiment analysis?

        It is an NLP task that decides what feeling a piece of text expresses: usually positive, negative or neutral.

        Why analyze YouTube comments?

        Comments show how real viewers feel. Creators and companies use them to find what people like, what annoys them and what to improve, without reading thousands of comments by hand.

        How does TF-IDF work?

        TF (term frequency) counts how often a word appears in a comment. IDF (inverse document frequency) lowers the weight of words that appear in almost every comment. Words that are frequent here but rare elsewhere get a high score.

        How does the model work?

        Logistic Regression learns a weight for every word during training. For a new comment it adds up the weights, turns the totals into probabilities for each class, and picks the most probable class.

        What do the labels mean?

        Positive praise or enjoyment. Negative criticism or dislike. Neutral questions, facts or comments without a clear opinion.
